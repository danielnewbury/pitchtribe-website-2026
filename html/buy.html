Do not pay extra for 4K. PitchTribe draws large text, a crest and a results panel, read from three metres or more. At that distance 1080p and 4K are the same picture, and the 1080p screen is £50 to £90 cheaper. Spend the difference on a duty rating and on brightness instead.

What to ask a reseller for: a 16/7 or 24/7 duty rating, 350 cd/m² or brighter, and portrait support if the wall you have in mind is tall and narrow. Those three decide whether a screen survives a clubhouse; the badge on the bezel does not.

Match the bracket to the weight and the hole spacing, not to the inch rating. Reckon on about 8 kg for a 43″ screen, 15 kg for a 55″ and 20 to 25 kg for a 65″, with signage panels a little heavier than televisions of the same size. The VESA hole pattern is printed in the manual and it is not always what the bracket box claims to cover.

And the failure nobody plans for: plasterboard with nothing solid behind it will not hold a 65″ screen on plasterboard plugs, however good the bracket is. That is the fixing, not the mount. Ten minutes with a stud detector before anyone drills is the cheapest part of the whole job. If the building is leased or listed and you are not allowed to drill at all, the floor stand or the trolley is the answer — worth knowing before you order a bracket.

Smart TV or signage panel?

The warranty

A television is warranted for use in a home. A clubhouse is commercial premises and ten hours a day is commercial use, and manufacturers do turn claims down on exactly that basis. Signage panels are sold for the job and usually carry three years.

It turns itself off

This is the one you will meet weekly. Televisions have eco timers and no-signal shutdown, and when power comes back they wake on the tuner rather than on HDMI. Somebody has to walk over with a remote. A signage panel wakes on the input you told it to and stays there.

A layout that never changes

The same header bar in the same place, all season. Signage panels shift their pixels slightly to cope with that and are rated for unchanging images; a cheap television left on one layout for a year can keep a ghost of it.

Brightness, if there is a window

Budget televisions run around 250 to 300 cd/m², which is fine on an inside wall. Facing a south-facing clubhouse window you want 500 cd/m² or more, and that is a high-brightness panel rather than a television at any price.

Turning it on its side

Only signage panels are rated to run portrait. A television mounted on its end traps its own heat and voids the warranty twice over, so if the wall you have is tall and narrow the choice is already made.

Home screens and adverts

Budget smart televisions increasingly boot to their own launcher, with adverts on it, and some nag for a button press before they carry on. A signage panel has no tuner and nothing to sell you.

What we would put in an average clubhouse

A 43″ signage panel on a tilting bracket — call it £400 and £60 — on top of the £173.70 Pi build. Roughly £635 for one screen, parts only, before any labour. If that is out of reach, a £249 43″ smart TV genuinely does work and we will happily put PitchTribe on one; you are trading away the warranty and accepting that somebody picks up a remote now and then.

Buy any of it yourself — nothing on this page pays us a penny, and we would rather you kept the difference. Or ask, and the screen and the mount go into one written quote alongside the unit and the fitting.

Be warned

What that £173.70 does not cover

The parts list is honest about the parts. These are the things that actually hold up an installation on the day, roughly in the order they bite.

A way to hang it

The official case has no VESA pattern, so you want a bracket or plate — otherwise the Pi ends up cable-tied behind the TV. Per screen. Mounts are priced above.

A mains socket near the screen

The official power lead is 1.5 m. Wall brackets are rarely next to a double socket, and this is the single most common reason an install stalls halfway.

Somewhere for the network lead to go

A 15 m lead assumes a spare port at the other end, and a route to get there. Wi-Fi avoids the problem entirely if the signal reaches.

The TV itself

Not in that total. If a screen has to be bought it will dwarf everything above it, and the TV is also where nearly all the running electricity goes — not the Pi. Screens are priced above, by size.

A spare memory card, and a backup stick

Cards are the usual failure on an always-on Pi, and a dead one at 2pm on a Saturday is the version of that you will meet. A second imaged card is cheap insurance. Add a small USB stick left in the Pi — a 64 GB SanDisk Ultra Fit was £10.49 on 4 November 2026 — and PitchTribe backs everything up to it every night, so the spare card is back to normal in ten minutes.

More screens, multiplied

One Pi drives up to two displays. Beyond that, each additional screen is another full build at the price above.

Clubs are commercial premises, so their insurer may also want anything plugged in to have been portable-appliance tested. Worth asking your committee before the day, not after.

Guest Wi-Fi, further

Wi-Fi for the whole venue

PitchTribe’s guest Wi-Fi runs on the Pi’s built-in radio, which covers about one room. For a clubhouse with a terrace, or a ground with a stand and a pitchside bar, add one of the two upgrades below. Guests get the same sign-in page, time limits and speed limits wherever they are, and you switch it all on from the dashboard. UK prices including VAT, checked 4 November 2026.
