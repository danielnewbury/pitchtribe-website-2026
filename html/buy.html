Be warned

What that £173.70 does not cover

The parts list is honest about the parts. These are the things that actually hold up an installation on the day, roughly in the order they bite.

A way to hang it

The official case has no VESA pattern, so you want a bracket or plate — otherwise the Pi ends up cable-tied behind the TV. Per screen.

A mains socket near the screen

The official power lead is 1.5 m. Wall brackets are rarely next to a double socket, and this is the single most common reason an install stalls halfway.

Somewhere for the network lead to go

A 15 m lead assumes a spare port at the other end, and a route to get there. Wi-Fi avoids the problem entirely if the signal reaches.

The TV itself

Not in that total. If a screen has to be bought it will dwarf everything above it, and the TV is also where nearly all the running electricity goes — not the Pi.

A spare memory card

Cards are the usual failure on an always-on Pi, and a dead one at 2pm on a Saturday is the version of that you will meet. A second imaged card is cheap insurance.

More screens, multiplied

One Pi drives up to two displays. Beyond that, each additional screen is another full build at the price above.

Clubs are commercial premises, so their insurer may also want anything plugged in to have been portable-appliance tested. Worth asking your committee before the day, not after.
